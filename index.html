Abstract

    The Sunshine Skyway Bridge, spanning Tampa Bay, Florida, is a critical infrastructure landmark
    with a tragic history.  In 1980, the original steel cantilever bridge collapsed after a freighter
    collided with a support pier, killing 35 people.This tragedy led to the bridge’s reconstruction
    as the world’s longest cable-stayed concrete bridge. The new design incorporated advanced safety 
    features, such as the Physical Oceanographic Real-Time System (PORTS) for weather and water
    condition monitoring, better bridge flexibility to absorb impacts, and reinforced structural 
    elements like rock islands and dolphins to protect against ship collisions.

Introduction

   The Sunshine Skyway bridge is currently the worlds longest cable-stayed concrete bridge, spanning
   Tampa Bay in Florida, USA. It’s critical to residents due to its role in connecting the gulf coast of
   Florida to the Tampa Bay area. It is known as a proud landmark of the Sunshine State, however its legacy 
   is tainted with tragedy, due to its collapse in 1980. This article explores the engineering behind 
   the rebuild of the bridge and the modern ingenious improvements made to prevent any more incidents.

Collapse of the first Sunshine Skyway

  Originally the bridge was a steel cantilever bridge, which are more vulnerable to collapse under large
  impact forces due to the fact that the bridge has no flex, meaning it cannot absorb any impacts and
  becomes very stressed under pressure. This vulnerability became evident on May 9th, 1980, when the MS 
  Summit Venture freighter ship collided with a support pier near the centre of the bridge, causing a 430m
  section of the bridge to collapse and fall into the bay below. Traffic plunged into the water below, leading
  to the deaths of 35 people. The event occurred during a severe thunderstorm when the crew lost control of the 
  ship. The event highlighted the need for the rebuild to include protection against both natural and physical 
  threats, with advanced weather warning systems implemented, and consideration into how to strengthen the 
  bridge against impacts.

Engineering an improved Sunshine Skyway

  The two key elements considered in the re-design was the prevention of another impact using advanced weather and 
  warning systems, and structural improvements, in case of another impact with the bridge.
  The new system implemented is called a ‘physical oceanographic real-time system’ (PORTS). The system includes sensors
  placed around the bridge and entire bay area to measure weather elements, such as windspeed, atmospheric pressure, 
  fog and air temperatures. Also, water elements, such as waves, currents, tides, water levels and water temperatures.
  All these factors affect how a ship travels, so the ports system will notify incoming ships of the conditions, so
  that they can adjust their approach towards the bridge.

  However, in cases where the collision cannot be avoided, the bridge’s redesign makes it much safer and less likely to 
  collapse. The bridge was redesigned to a cable-stayed bridge, 21 steel cables encased in steel piping 22cm in diameter 
  support the roadway. Which allows for much better distribution of force across the bridge. Also, the cables mean the 
  bridge is much more flexible than the rigid structure of a cantilever bridge. These both mean the bridge is much less 
  likely to collapse, as it can absorb impacts better than a solid structure like a cantilever bridge. The changed design 
  also allowed the clearance height to be increased, allowing larger vessels to travel underneath but also lowering the 
  risk from storm surges. These are a frequent concern in Florida, due to its high hurricane activity, such as the recent 
  Hurricane Milton. 

  Other structural improvements include the addition of rock islands, dolphins, stronger crash barriers, reflective markers
  and a modern lighting system. Rock islands float on the water surrounding the supports of the bridge, the rock island would
  reduce the impact with the actual supports of the bridge, as it would slow the ship before impact. Dolphins are large 
  cylindrical bumpers, 20m in diameter, that stick down into the waterbed below. 36 bumpers were built along each side of the 
  main span of the bridge, made with concrete and filled with rock, each can withstand the impact of an 87,000-ton ship.

Did it work?

  The rebuild was completed in 1987 by the American Bridge Company, designed by Figg & Miller bridges. It is the worlds longest
  cable-stayed concrete bridge at 6663m long, its longest span is 365m and the roadway sits at 60m above water. The rebuilt cost 
  $244 million and took seven years. In October 2024, the Tampa Bay area was hit directly by hurricane Milton, the second most 
  intense Atlantic hurricane ever recorded. However, the sunshine skyway suffered zero damage. The bridge has also been struck by 
  ships since the rebuild, such as a cargo ship in 1987, however due to the reinforced design of the bridge it suffered minimal damage.

Conclusion

  Through modern technologies, and a commitment to safety, no matter the extra costs, the engineers created a bridge that Florida can
  be proud of. A bridge that, despite its tragic history, people can feel safe crossing and ships can feel safe passing through. They
  learned from the collapse, and implemented improvements that directly respond to it, such as the PORTS system to counter bad weather 
  conditions, and many structural improvements to reduce impacts. Thanks to engineering, the rebuild of the Sunshine Skyway has proven 
  to be a huge success.
